Here is the full breakdown of **Teeny Titans 2** (officially titled ***Teen Titans GO Figure!***).

This game builds heavily on the original *Teeny Titans* formula: a lighthearted, figure-collecting RPG that blends 3v3 real-time battle bar mechanics with open-world DC exploration.

---

## Game Overview & Progression (Start to End)

* **Avatar Selection:** At the start of the game, you choose one of five Teen Titans as your overworld avatar: **Robin, Raven, Cyborg, Starfire, or Beast Boy**. Your choice changes overworld dialogue, special character-driven interactions, and unique voice acting.
* **The Plot:** Someone is attempting to put the Teeny Titans toy business out of operation with counterfeit figures and shady sales tactics. You travel through the DC universe to uncover who is behind the conspiracy, earn championship badges, and reign supreme in competitive battle leagues.
* **The Core Loop:**

$$\text{Explore Cities} \rightarrow \text{Buy/Find Figures} \rightarrow \text{Level/Upgrade Team} \rightarrow \text{Win Local Tournaments} \rightarrow \text{Unlock New Areas/Cities}$$


* **End Game:** After defeating regional tournament champions across Jump City, Gotham City, and Metropolis, you battle through elite tournaments (like the Justice League or Legion of Doom events) to catch every collectible figure and claim the title of Mega Teeny Champion.

---

## Map & Exploration

Unlike the original game (which took place entirely in Jump City), *Teeny Titans 2* expands across three major DC hub worlds:

* **Jump City:** The Titans' home turf featuring iconic spots like Titans Tower, Zippy's Pizza, and Lil' Alfred's Toy Cave.
* **Gotham City:** A darker, rainier urban map featuring locations like the Batcave, Arkham Asylum, and Gotham toy stores.
* **Metropolis:** A bright, high-rise city featuring LexCorp, the Daily Planet, and the Hall of Justice.

### Exploration Features

* **Fast Travel:** Move quickly between neighborhoods and cities using sewer drains, space cabs, or transport stations.
* **Dynamic Day/Night & Weather Systems:** Time continuously advances as you walk and battle. Certain stores close at night, while specific shady characters, nocturnal figures, or midnight tournaments only appear after dark.
* **Scavenging:** You can tap trash cans, trees, water bodies, and street corners to collect dropped coins, free item boxes, tofu, or rare **Mod Chips**.
* **NPC Interactions & Side Quests:** Dozens of classic DC characters (from Batman, Superman, and Wonder Woman down to niche villains like Condiment King) wander the streets. You can speak to them to receive quests or challenge them to duels.

---

## Battle Mechanics

Combat is a **3v3 active-time battle (ATB)** system played out in real-time using a shared **Battle Bar**.

[ Active Figure ]  <--->  [ Shared Battle Bar ]  <--->  [ Enemy Figure ]
                         | Slot 1 | Slot 2 | Slot 3 |


### The Battle Bar & Switching

* **Shared Power:** Both you and your opponent have a single Battle Bar that constantly fills up from left to right in real-time.
* **3 Active Slot Team:** You equip up to 6 figures in your total deck, but bring **3 figures into a match**. Only one figure fights on screen at a time.
* **Instant Switching:** Tapping another figure on your bench swaps them into battle instantly. The Battle Bar **does not reset** when you swap, allowing you to charge up energy with a tanky figure and swap to a glass-cannon figure to spend it immediately.

### Abilities & Attack Types

Every figure has **3 distinct ability slots** mapped directly to specific segments on the Battle Bar:

* **Slot 1 (Low Cost):** Quick, weak attacks or basic utility moves.
* **Slot 2 (Medium Cost):** Balanced attacks, group damage, or status buffs.
* **Slot 3 (High Cost):** High-damage ultimate attacks, multi-target hits, or heavy crowd-control effects.

**Key Ability Variations:**

* **Charge-Up (Purple Moves):** Require holding the bar at that spot for a few seconds before firing. They deal huge damage but risk being interrupted if you take damage.
* **Status Effects:** Moves can inflict Stun, Freeze (stops the opponent's bar), Burn/Poison (damage over time), Bar Depletion (drains opponent's bar), or Dodge Boosts.
* **Tofu Drops:** Some abilities drop edible Tofu, which grants random active buffs, health recovery, or bar speed boosts.

### Battle Accessories

Before a fight, you equip one team accessory (e.g., the **Bat-Signal**, **Superman's Underpants**, or **Green Lantern Ring**). Over the course of the match, an accessory meter slowly builds up. Once full, activating it triggers a match-altering effect, such as team healing or massive burst damage.

---

## Shops & Economy

Shops are scattered everywhere, each specializing in different figure categories, chip types, or accessories:

* **Inventory Rotation:** Shops restock their inventories as the in-game days cycle.
* **Specialized Stores:**
* *Bizarro Comics:* Focuses on villain and weird variant figures.
* *Toyman / Lil' Alfred's:* High-tier hero stock.
* *Rainbow Raider:* Sells figure **Repaints** to customize paint jobs and alter cosmetic variants.
* *24-Hour Shop & Sticky Joe:* Sells budget items, mystery boxes, and random consumables.



---

## Upgrades, Mod Chips, and Customization

Managing and upgrading your 100+ figure roster takes place inside **Titans Tower**:

### 1. Stats & Leveling

Figures have four core stats: **Health, Damage, Luck (Critical Hit Chance), and Dodge (Chance to avoid damage entirely)**. Figures level up from 1 to 20 by gaining EXP in combat or by consuming duplicate figures and Tofu.

### 2. Gold Upgrades & Slot Customization

* **Slot Reordering:** You can pay coins to change which slot an ability sits in. Moving a figure's best move from Slot 3 to Slot 1 allows you to spam it much faster (at the cost of base damage tweaks).
* **Gold Upgrades:** Investing coins into an ability turns its icon gold, adding bonus effects like guaranteed critical hits, added stuns, or zero bar cost.

### 3. Mod Chips

Once a figure reaches **Level 5**, you can attach a **Mod Chip** to the bottom of its base. A figure can only hold one chip at a time, and equipping a new chip destroys the old one.

* **Stat Boost Mods:** Directly raise one stat (e.g., +20% Dodge) or lower one stat to heavily boost another.
* **Battle Effect Mods:** Grant automatic passive buffs at match start, such as starting with a full accessory meter or cursing the opponent's bar speed.
* **Archetype Mods:** Exclusive perks based on figure type (e.g., health regeneration on *Cute* category figures).

End-to-end walkthrough of the game concept
This is the complete design direction that has emerged from the conversation, including the original idea, the systems added later, the technical architecture, the asset pipeline, the intended MVP, and the areas still requiring decisions.

The most important high-level conclusion is:

This is a collectible squad battler with real-time battle-bar combat, character progression, a store, missions, tournaments, multiple story campaigns, and a distinctive static-image presentation built around animated rectangular and square panels.

It deliberately borrows some of the appealing structural ideas from Teeny Titans, especially the battle bar, three-action characters, figure collection, upgrades, types, shops, tournaments and mod-style customisation. It deliberately removes open-world walking and exploration. The supplied Teeny Titans 2 breakdown is useful as system inspiration, but its cities, roaming NPCs, scavenging, day/night exploration and physical traversal are not part of this game. 


The earlier imported discussion also established that the visual limitation should become the game’s intentional style: static rectangular artwork, image swaps, zooms, slides, shake, hit-stop, flashes, sound and two-frame character motion instead of conventional animation. 


1. The original problem
The original starting point was that conventional game animation felt like a major barrier.

You had already worked out a battle concept, but you did not want to become an animator or depend on large traditional sprite sets. You were considering:

You also wanted a progression structure that did not require a fully explorable world.

A Candy Crush-style level path was initially considered because it is easy to understand and easy to extend, but you correctly identified the risks:

That removes the need for a walkable map without reducing the game to a meaningless sequence of fights.

The most important confirmed decisions are now:

Two battle bars, one per side/team.

1v1 controller structure, supporting human vs bot, human vs human and bot vs bot.

Squads contain 1 to 3 active battle choices, with one character on-screen at a time.

Switching is free, preserves the team bar and cannot dodge an already committed attack.

Actions may prevent switching temporarily.

Team-wide damage, healing and stun affect the whole squad, including benched characters.

Benched statuses continue ticking.

Battles end through full team defeat or forfeit.

Charge attacks exist and can be interrupted.

Multi-hit actions roll dodge and critical results per hit.

Each action can be assigned to nine bar positions: 1L, 1, 1H, 2L, 2, 2H, 3L, 3, 3H.

Moving an action earlier makes it faster and weaker; moving it later makes it slower and stronger.

Every character has three action upgrade tiers: stock, gold and platinum.

Action configuration and upgrades unlock at level 10.

Modifications unlock at level 5.

Character level cap is 25.

Stat points are freely reallocatable outside battle.

Exact duplicate characters are allowed and purchasable.

Variants are primarily recolours/reskins of the same character.

There are six classes, plus optional untyped characters.

Class effectiveness uses the character class, not the action.

A second grouping dimension exists, such as hero/villain or another original equivalent, and that grouping creates squad bonuses.

Smaller battle squads concentrate XP across fewer participating characters.

Story missions may force squads or loan characters.

Tournaments carry health and defeated state between rounds.

Tournament rosters are larger than battle squads, probably around seven or eight characters, with up to three selected per fight.

Tournament modifications are locked once the tournament begins.

Tournament intermissions can contain healing, revival and temporary next-round bonuses.

Quick Fight and standalone Tournament Mode allow unrestricted characters, levels, upgrades and configurations.

Story versions of tournaments use the same tournament engine and fixed authored configurations.

AI can switch strategically and sees the same visible information as the player.

Easy is highly forgiving; Normal allows inattentive losses; Hard is meaningfully challenging; Brutal expects very strong execution.

Local saves, multiple profiles, separate settings and progression, and frequent autosaving are confirmed.

The player is a named collector competing to become the ultimate collector.

Decisions I would make from your delegated answers
You explicitly left several things to design judgement. These are the defaults I would document unless playtesting disproves them.

Defence
Do not make Defence a sixth allocatable stat.

Use five allocatable stats:

Power

Health

Speed

Dodge

Luck

Defence is a temporary battle state or derived modifier affected by:

actions

power-ups

modifications

game modes

tournament rewards

When Defence is broken or reduced, incoming damage rises and dodge may be disabled. This matches how you described it without adding another permanent stat to optimise.

Evasion cap
Dodge should use diminishing returns and a hard cap, probably around 40–50% before temporary battle effects.

Power and Health should remain the safest and most consistently rewarding allocations, as requested.

XP distribution
For a normal fight:

only the selected battle squad receives XP

all selected characters receive XP, including defeated characters

XP is split across the selected squad

using fewer characters therefore levels them faster

the rest of the collection receives no passive XP

This is understandable and creates a deliberate reason to use smaller squads.

Loss rewards
A loss should provide:

partial XP

no standard currency reward

mission progress only for objectives that logically occurred, such as total damage or using an action

no progress for objectives requiring victory

This avoids making a loss pointless while keeping victory meaningful.

Duplicate character use
Duplicates are a permanent collection mechanic.

Each owned copy has its own:

level

XP

stat allocation

action upgrades

equipped modification

cosmetic variant

A recolour remains attached to that owned copy but does not alter its class or mechanics.

Upgrade materials
Do not require consuming duplicates for action upgrades initially.

Use currency for upgrades. Duplicate consumption creates pressure to buy duplicate figures and introduces unnecessary economy complexity. It can be evaluated later as an optional sink if the store produces too many duplicates.

Squad synergy
Keep these two systems separate:

Class effectiveness
The six-class wheel determines strong and weak matchups.

Affiliation synergy
Characters also have an affiliation or alignment tag, such as heroic, villainous, rogue or another branded vocabulary.

Only matching affiliation counts create a squad bonus. One character alone does not need a meaningful “synergy” reward. The useful thresholds should be:

2 matching affiliation characters → tier 1 bonus
3 matching affiliation characters → tier 2 bonus
Exact duplicate characters may receive an additional optional set bonus later, but not in the MVP.

Battle time limit
Use 90 seconds as the default, configurable per encounter.

At timeout:

compare surviving total HP percentage

higher percentage wins

if tied, compare total remaining HP

if still tied, declare a draw or use a short sudden-death period

The timer should rarely decide normal matches.

Quick Fight rewards
Split it explicitly:

Quick Fight

unrestricted roster and configurations

modest currency reward

no character XP

no story mission progress

Practice

unrestricted

no rewards

intended for testing builds and characters

That avoids unrestricted max-level configurations becoming the best story grinding method.

Tournament Mode
Standalone Tournament Mode uses unrestricted characters and upgrades, but has its own tournament score and rewards rather than changing Story Mode character progression.

Story tournaments use the player’s actual owned collection and persistent progression.

Both use the same underlying tournament engine.

Difficulty rewards
Keep Easy and Normal rewards identical.

Hard and Brutal do not need larger rewards. The sarcastic commentary, completion records, badges and statistics can provide the recognition. This avoids making players feel financially compelled to choose difficulty.

AI fairness
AI should follow the same combat rules.

Difficulty changes:

reaction delay

strategic planning depth

willingness to switch

action selection quality

ability to plan around visible bar values

tolerance for mistakes

possibly modest authored stat modifiers

Avoid hidden cheating on Brutal unless a fight explicitly announces a special rule.

Story character overrides
A story may loan temporary characters or create a temporary squad.

It never removes or modifies the player’s owned characters. Temporary story versions exist only for that node or sequence.

Loadouts
A saved character loadout should contain:

stat allocation

action slot arrangement

equipped modification ID

A saved squad should contain:

owned character instance IDs

character order

selected character loadouts

optional accessory or future team item
