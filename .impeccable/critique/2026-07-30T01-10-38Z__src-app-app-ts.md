## Design Specificity Verdict

The battle is authored, not generic—but it is authored around the wrong centre of gravity. The Collector Drawer is specific to loftwah/fighter, yet the interface feels like operating a collection-management surface while a fight happens inside one compartment. The arena should be the spectacle and the UI should behave like comic-book editing around it. Instead, static rails and readouts retain equal visual weight throughout the fight.

The implementation is faithful to the current visual contract. `DESIGN.md`, `.impeccable/notes/battle-surface-brief.md`, and the approved Collector Drawer mock explicitly require the three-column drawer, opposing rails, and lower Charge Strip. The failure is therefore specification-level, not simply weak CSS execution. The rejected direction should be replaced in `DESIGN.md` once a new direction is accepted.

## Overall Impression

The battle is readable and technically disciplined, but emotionally flat. It opens as a dense control surface, immediately begins simulating, and offers only a narrow set of short visual reactions. The central opportunity is to replace “collector drawer being operated” with “comic issue being cut live around the fight.”

## What Is Working

- The domain/presentation boundary is excellent raw material. Semantic events already separate deterministic combat from Phaser, so a richer presentation director can be added without putting game rules inside rendering.
- Core controls are semantic buttons with keyboard access, visible text state, `aria-disabled`, predicted output, touch-sized targets, reduced-motion support, and a semantic Charge meter.
- The raster inventory is structurally useful: 4:5 character stills are crop-safe for close-ups, the idle pairs support frame swaps, and Mara's three 16:9 Move images prove the cut-in pipeline.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The fight has no start ritual

**Why it matters:** `mountBattleGame()` hides “Preparing print” and immediately sets `#battleReady = true`. The timer, Charge, and AI begin on the next animation frame. There is no anticipation, no clear boundary between loading and live play, and no moment that frames both fighters as opponents.

**Fix:** Add an application-owned pre-fight phase: `loading → countdown → active`. Stage **3**, **2**, **1**, **FIGHT** over roughly 2.5–3 seconds. Do not tick elapsed time, Charge, statuses, pending Moves, or AI until `FIGHT`. Reset the AI decision clock at that moment. Reduced motion should use static swaps rather than zoom and shake.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable animate`

### [P1] The presentation system is a reaction list, not choreography

**Why it matters:** `BattleScene.present()` responds visually to only `damageApplied`, `actionStarted`, `characterSwitched`, and `characterDefeated`. It does not author distinct beats for `actionCharged`, interruption, dodge, critical hit, stun/status, or battle start. `presentCutIn()` runs only for the player, and only three of eighteen Moves have non-null presentation assets. Every non-Mara Move therefore skips the intended cut-in completely.

**Fix:** Add an event-driven Presentation Director that queues short beats: anticipation, cut-in, impact/hit-stop, reaction, and recovery. Give every semantic event a default composition that works with existing character stills, then allow content-defined overrides. Use crops, duplicate layers, panel masks, translation, scale, rotation, flashes, typography, and screen-edge entrances before commissioning more bespoke art.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable overdrive`

### [P1] Charge cadence and Move value do not have a declared balance model

**Why it matters:** Charge fills at `8 × (1 + Tempo × 0.025)`. Across the current Tempo range of 3–9, that is only 8.6–9.8 Charge per second—a roughly 14% spread—so Tempo barely changes tactical cadence. The player normally starts at 22 while the enemy defaults to 10. A Tempo-5 player reaches a 25-cost Move about 0.33 seconds after the fight starts, undermining the bar's anticipation. From the same opening state, 50 Charge arrives in roughly 3.1 seconds and 75 in 5.9 seconds.

The value ladder is not consistently more efficient at higher commitment. Before level, Power, class, tier, and variance, Mara's quick Move yields about 0.590 nominal damage per Charge while her three-hit finisher yields about 0.588. Knuckle Tax falls from about 0.558 to 0.504. Higher-cost Moves can still provide burst or utility, but the content has no explicit effect budget proving that trade. Charge-up durations range from 0 to 1.35 seconds without documented cadence targets. Tests assert that later positions cost more and are stronger, but do not benchmark first-action timing, 30-second output, Tempo identity, AI fairness, or dominated Moves.

**Fix:** Adopt explicit cadence targets before changing numbers. Recommended first prototype:

- symmetric base opening Charge of 12, with authored bonuses applied explicitly;
- fill rate `7 + Tempo × 0.35`, producing 8.05–10.15 Charge per second across Tempo 3–9;
- keep the current nine cost positions for the first experiment so timing changes are isolated;
- target a typical Tempo-5 readiness curve of about 1.5 seconds to cost 25, 4.3 seconds to cost 50, and 7.2 seconds to cost 75 from the opening state;
- define utility budgets for damage, healing, stun, defence, Charge manipulation, area targeting, wind-up risk, and interruption;
- add seeded 10-second and 30-second rotation benchmarks for every Relic and difficulty.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] The visual hierarchy makes the UI the subject and the fight the content

**Why it matters:** The battle comp gives persistent mass to the top rail, two bench rails, two readouts, matchup stamp, combat log, and Charge deck. The arena is boxed in, while the paper treatments and labels all shout at comparable volume. The result is dense but not kinetic. The three existing battle mocks are variations inside the same risograph-dashboard/collector-drawer premise, not genuinely different experiential directions.

**Fix:** Replace the battle composition rather than reskin it. Keep rules readable through a restrained semantic DOM layer, but let the arena occupy 65–75% of the first viewport and allow panels to temporarily cover, crop, split, and leave the stage in response to events. Collapse bench information until switching is relevant.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] The Charge Strip is technically integrated but visually awkward

**Why it matters:** Absolute-positioned circular seals sit over a segmented rectangular meter, surrounded by tiny state, tier, output, key, name, and tooltip labels. It communicates everything, but it looks like controls pinned over a progress component rather than a single authored combat instrument. Repeated later CSS overrides for `.battle-drawer`, `.player-charge-deck`, `.command-charge-meter`, and the Move tooltip also make the final responsive result fragile.

**Fix:** Replace the seals with three rectangular comic-gutter Move tabs embedded in one thick “Impact Rail.” Fill should behave like ink flooding underneath the tabs; each threshold should tear or stamp from `N TO GO` to `READY`. Preserve equal prominence, visible names/costs/predictions, keyboard shortcuts, and text-plus-colour state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Concrete Direction Options

### Option A — Kinetic Comic Broadcast (recommended)

The default view is a large, clean duel stage with compact health and Charge at the edges. Semantic events cut to aggressive close-ups, diagonal crops, reaction strips, impact typography, and brief split screens, then snap back to the readable fight. It reuses almost all current stills and can provide a generic choreography for every Move immediately.

**Trade-off:** Medium implementation effort; strongest balance of spectacle, clarity, asset reuse, and responsive behavior.

### Option B — Living Comic Page

The entire battle is a page whose panel grid reconfigures: wide establishing panel, two portrait gutters for charge-up, inset reaction panel for dodge or stun, full-bleed splash for finishers. Panels physically enter and leave as events resolve.

**Trade-off:** Most comic-book-specific and memorable, but highest layout, sequencing, reduced-motion, and mobile complexity.

### Option C — Toy-Fight Motion Cinema

Keep a stable full-bleed arena, but turn stills into cinematic subjects through continuous reframing: slow camera drift, parallax print layers, silhouette wipes, foreground crops, zoom punches, screen-edge entrances, reaction close-ups, and strong hit-stop. UI becomes a restrained broadcast lower-third rather than a drawer.

**Trade-off:** Fastest and safest presentation upgrade, but less explicitly “comic page” than Options A or B.

### Recommendation

Use **Option A as the base system**, with **Option B's dynamic panel layouts reserved for countdowns, finishers, critical hits, defeats, and story-authored moments**. This gives every ordinary Move consistent energy without making the whole fight unreadable.

The opening sequence should establish that language immediately:

1. **3** — player portrait panel slams in;
2. **2** — enemy portrait panel answers from the opposite side;
3. **1** — both panels compress into a versus split;
4. **FIGHT** — the centre gutter tears open into the live arena.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time player:** The exact Move costs and readiness are visible, but nothing explains why the bar starts partly filled, what Tempo changes, or why a higher-cost Move is worth waiting for. The fight begins before Jordan has parsed the dense screen.

**Alex, power player:** Keyboard shortcuts are good, but weak Tempo differentiation and inconsistent damage-per-Charge ratios make optimisation feel shallow. Characters without presentation assets also feel unfinished regardless of mechanical depth.

**Sam, accessibility-dependent player:** Semantic controls and reduced motion are strong. Risks remain: the narrow layout hides the visible “TIME” label, Move tooltips lack an explicit `aria-describedby` relationship, and future countdown/motion sequences will need non-visual announcements and no time-limited input.

## Minor Observations

- The enemy AI reaction delay changes sharply by difficulty but is not explicitly designed alongside Charge readiness, so difficulty can alter apparent fairness rather than only decision quality.
- Fighter readouts are rebuilt with `innerHTML` on the throttled UI cycle even when much of their content is unchanged, adding avoidable DOM churn beside Phaser.
- The current Mara cut-in stretches a 16:9 image to the full arena, slides it in over 150ms, holds for 160ms, then reverses. That is a single transition, not a reusable comic grammar.
- The result stamp has a stronger peak than most of the fight. The emotional high point should be the decisive Move, with the result acting as release.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the battle feel like a comic being edited live, or like collectible toys being filmed cinematically?
- Is the archive/risograph identity still wanted outside battle, or has the whole product direction been rejected?
- Should Charge reward patient commitment to finishers, rapid tactical cycling, or momentum generated by landing hits?
