# Art direction questionnaire

Status: working interview. These questions do not change the visual system or
content until the answers are reviewed and deliberately adopted in `DESIGN.md`
and the authored content.

Answer briefly, skip anything you do not care about, and add references wherever
words are inadequate.

## Batch 1 — visual world, characters, and attacks

1. What three adjectives should describe the game the instant a battle appears?
2. What three adjectives should the game never feel like?
3. Should the overall tone be mostly cute, cool, funny, dramatic, strange,
   sinister, sincere, or some deliberate mixture?
4. Who should the game feel made for: young children, older children, teenagers,
   nostalgic adults, broad family play, or another audience?
5. How sharp can the humour be? Choose a boundary: gentle cartoon jokes,
   mischievous satire, absurdist comedy, meaner rivalry, or mostly serious.
6. Name up to five games, cartoons, comics, films, artists, toys, or brands whose
   visual feeling you genuinely want near this project.
7. Name up to five references that resemble the current work but represent the
   wrong direction.
8. From Teeny Titans 2, which qualities do you want to preserve beyond the
   battle layout: proportions, colour, animation timing, character appeal,
   humour, clarity, toy collecting, or something else?
9. Should characters look like chibi figures, stylised superheroes, anime
   fighters, vinyl toys, hand-drawn cartoons, 3D collectibles, or another form?
10. How exaggerated should bodies be: tiny body/large head, compact cartoon,
    heroic proportions, lanky and strange, or varied by character?
11. Which image treatment feels best: clean vector shapes, cel-shaded anime,
    painted illustration, inked comic panels, textured printmaking, polished 3D,
    or mixed media?
12. Should outlines be heavy and graphic, thin and clean, sketchy and lively, or
    mostly absent?
13. Should the palette feel candy-bright, Saturday-morning colourful,
    comic-primary, pastel, muted and tactile, dark with bright accents, or
    something else?
14. Which three to six colours do you naturally want to see most often?
15. Are there colours or combinations you actively dislike or associate with
    the wrong genre?
16. Should battles sit on mostly light, mostly dark, or scene-dependent
    backgrounds?
17. What should the interface feel physically made from: toy packaging, comic
    panels, arcade plastic, stickers, trading cards, notebooks, television
    graphics, clean digital shapes, or another material language?
18. Should the cast inhabit one coherent world, or should wildly different
    genres and visual styles collide because they are collectible figures?
19. What should the cast contain: mostly humans, superheroes, monsters, robots,
    animals, magical beings, objects brought to life, or an intentional mix?
20. What makes a character instantly collectible to you: silhouette, costume,
    face, colour scheme, weapon, transformation, personality, rarity, lore, or
    another hook?
21. Do you prefer character names that sound heroic, funny, toy-commercial,
    mysterious, anime-styled, ordinary-but-strange, or something else?
22. Which attack fantasies belong in the game: martial arts, superpowers,
    elemental magic, gadgets, weapons, summons, slapstick, psychic effects,
    transformations, or another set?
23. Which current attack themes or names feel especially wrong, embarrassing,
    generic, or out of place?
24. How should attacks communicate impact: comic words and panels, anime speed
    lines, squash-and-stretch, camera cuts, toy collisions, elemental spectacle,
    cleaner tactical feedback, or a selected combination?
25. If we generate one hero, one rival, one arena, and three attacks to prove a
    replacement direction, what should that small test scene be about?
